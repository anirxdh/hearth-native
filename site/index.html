The big pictureone diagram

How a bottle becomes a calm morning

Everything in RUA flows from two facts printed on every prescription label: the fill date and the day supply. A human verifies them once; deterministic code does everything else. No AI touches a number, and nothing leaves the phone.

Stage 1 · Foundation & risk retirementP1 – P4

Before features: prove the scary parts

Four things could invalidate the whole architecture later: losing someone's data, an ugly design language, OCR not being good enough, and the legal reality of holding another person's health information. Stage 1 retired all four.

Phase 1

The encrypted vault & the durability gate

4 days · committed

Everything a caregiver will ever type lives in one SQLite database on the phone, encrypted with a key that never leaves the iOS Keychain. Before a single feature existed, this phase had to prove data survives crashes, upgrades, and even failed upgrades.

  • pnpm monorepo: the app, a pure-code engine package, a schema package — the engine can never secretly depend on UI
  • SQLCipher encryption: 256-bit key generated on first launch, stored only in the Keychain; the database file is unreadable without it
  • Copy-restore migrations: before any upgrade, the database is snapshotted; a failed upgrade restores it automatically
  • Every table carries UUIDv7 ids, UTC timestamps, and an append-only change log — the groundwork sync will use in Stage 7
  • Boot recovery screens for the two nightmare states (key lost, file lost) instead of a crash loop
Proved by: 22 database tests including a deliberately failing migration that rolls back losslessly; the app builds and boots on an iPhone 16e simulator with the encrypted database live.
the key never touches the file; the file never upgrades without a safety copy

Phase 3 · spike

Can a phone read a pill bottle? GO

3 days · committed

Before committing to the scan-a-label flow (Phase 21), we proved it. Forty realistic pharmacy labels — four layout families, rotations, glare, stickers covering fields, small type — were run through Apple's Vision OCR (the same engine iPhones use) and a deterministic parser, scored field by field against ground truth.

FieldClean labelsAll 40 labelsWhat failures looked like
Drug name100%100%none

Why this matters: zero wrong values in the whole run. Every failure was an absent field — exactly what a confirm-screen wants, because a blank asks the human while a wrong value sneaks past them. The scorecard now dictates which fields get low-confidence flags in the future scan UI. Final validation on your real bottles happens at Phase 21.

Phase 4 · spike

Whose data is it? The consent framework

2 days · committed

RUA holds health information about someone other than the phone's owner. Before building anything that stores it, this phase fixed the legal posture and wrote the exact words the app ships: the caregiver-authority attestation, the disclaimer set, and the intended-use line every future feature is checked against.

  • Legal posture, written down: not a HIPAA covered entity (and never claims it); FTC health-breach rules with a 60-day incident plan; Washington and California consumer-health laws mapped to concrete duties
  • The attestation: when you add a person, you confirm you have their permission or authority — saved with a timestamp on their record
  • Four disclaimers, verbatim: onboarding, runway screens, emergency guides, and the future assistant
  • The intended-use statement: an organizational tool — no dosing, no diagnosis, no triage, ever
  • Per-category consent scopes and respite-mode rules, ready for the sharing features in Stage 7
Proved by: the attestation and disclaimers are already live in the app — the add-person screen won't save without the confirmation, and every med screen carries the inventory-arithmetic footnote.
consent captured at the door · every sensitive view logged · words fixed before features

Phase 6

The runway engine — the deterministic heart

2 days · committed

One sum, done perfectly: the label says filled June 15, 30-day supply, so the supply ends July 15 — full stop. No AI, no adherence guessing, no clock tricks. If there's no fill on record, RUA says so instead of inventing a number.

  • runway = fill date + day supply − today, computed in pure code with no access to the system clock
  • Statuses live in code: OK → worth a look at 10 days → act now at 3 → overdue at 0
  • Med-sync calculator: finds the one pickup day that aligns a whole regimen, with short-fill quantities
  • Honesty rules: as-needed meds get no forecast; missing fills get an explicit "add fill" state
Proved by: a golden table of hand-computed cases pinned in tests — daylight-saving weekends, leap days, year boundaries, zero supplies, future fill dates. The math cannot drift a single day.
two numbers from the label place every medication on a number line — nothing to guess
Phase 7

Today's Digest — one answer per morning

3 days · committed

Opening RUA answers a single question: what is the one thing that needs me today? A ranking — not a feed — funnels every signal across every person into one lead action, with the calm shown quietly underneath. The ranking is code, so "why is this first?" always has an answer.

  • The funnel: overdue > urgent > soon > missing-fill > gone-quiet, ties broken by days left, then by high-alert
  • Exactly one lead card with its action; at most four quiet lines; a genuine "all good" state
  • Care-circle cards roll each person up to Act now / Worth a look / All good
  • Recomputes on every write and at day rollover — never a stale morning
Proved by: fixture households in tests pin the exact ranking order, and the screenshot shows the real seeded morning: Robert's metformin leads, everyone else stays quiet.
Phase 8

Refill alerts that survive a closed app

3 days · committed

Because the runway is arithmetic, every future reminder is knowable the moment a fill is saved — so RUA schedules them all with iOS immediately instead of hoping a background task runs someday. iOS background execution is famously unreliable; RUA simply doesn't depend on it.

  • Three alerts per medication — a heads-up (your chosen lead time), an urgent at 3 days, and run-out day — plus a next-morning nudge if it's already overdue
  • Write-time reconciliation: every med edit and app-open diffs the desired plan against what iOS actually has pending, canceling and scheduling only the difference
  • Quiet hours (even windows that wrap midnight), per-person toggles, and the iOS 64-pending cap handled nearest-first
  • Lock-screen privacy by rule and by test: payloads never contain a drug, dose, or name — "A medication needs attention"
  • Tapping an alert deep-links straight to the medication it is about
Proved by: planner unit tests for every window and edge; on the simulator, the seeded family yields 22 scheduled alerts (visible in Settings). The 3-day force-killed soak on a real device is yours to run per the roadmap.

Phases 14–16

Paper: one-pager, ER sheet, everyone pack, guides

8 days · committed

Print is the emergency interface. A pure template package renders US-Letter documents with the severe-allergy band leading every page; the app enforces two rules in one chokepoint — every print or share is logged, and PDFs live only in cache and are deleted after the share sheet closes. The one-pager adds a personalized daily-needs table (protein, fluids, fiber, and the rest) computed from published reference values by deterministic, tested rules — general guidance, framed as exactly that. Six emergency guides ship inside the binary with 24 hand-drawn pictograms: falls, choking, stroke, chest pain, bleeding, sudden confusion.

  • Templates are snapshot-tested and escape hostile input (a patient literally named <script> prints harmlessly)
  • The ER sheet opens two taps from home; call-first guides carry a red Call 911 pill
  • Every guide wears its review stamp on the surface: "Pending clinician review v0.9" until a licensed reviewer signs — honesty over theater
Proved by: 11 print snapshot tests, 11 nutrition tests; every document and guide opens and print-previews in airplane mode per the basement checklist.

Stage 4 · HardeningP17 – P19 (code)

Trustworthy is a feature

Before new capabilities, the app earned its keep: accessible to caregivers who use VoiceOver, honest when something breaks, recoverable when a phone is lost, and transparent about every time care data leaves the app.

Phases 17–19

Shields, backup, and the access log

10 days · committed

A privacy shield covers the screen with plain paper the instant RUA leaves the foreground, so the iOS app switcher never shows a medication list. An opt-in Face ID lock guards opening. Backup is the canonical SQLCipher move — the whole record re-keyed to a passphrase you choose, one file, restorable on a new phone — which also answers the deliberate "key loss means data loss" design with an escape hatch you control. And the access log turns a promise into a screen: every print, share, and export, timestamped, append-only.

  • Error boundary: no crash ever strands a caregiver on a white screen; data stays put, one tap retries
  • Accessibility: roles and labels on every interactive element, 44pt targets, chart alt text; the manual VoiceOver run is on the physical-device list
  • Delete-everything wipes the database, its key, the WAL files, and the document vault
  • HOSPITAL_BASEMENT.md: the airplane-mode release gate, committed to the repo and run before every release
Proved by: the full suite green; backup round-trip verified in code review against SQLCipher's documented export path; the basement checklist is now a standing release gate.

Architecture decisionsthe load-bearing choices

Decisions we'll still be glad about at Phase 32

The full record lives in ARCHITECTURE.md in the repo — fifteen decision records with costs and payoffs. The eight that matter most:

A React-free engine packageAll safety-critical math lives in pure TypeScript with 135 tests and no UI imports — reusable by the future server, Android, and auditors.
Encryption by constructionSQLCipher with the key in the Keychain: the file is unreadable off-device, and "we never see your data" is physics, not policy.
Two kinds of timeCalendar facts (fill dates) never mix with instants (timestamps); all day math is DST-proof by design, and the engine never reads the clock.
An append-only change logOne audit spine powers the history timeline, the consent story, and — unchanged — the Stage-7 sync engine. Sync becomes a migration, not a rewrite.
The database is the only stateScreens read rows and re-read on writes. No mirrored stores, no "the screen says 4 but the DB says 3."
Alerts scheduled at write timeEvery future reminder is computed the moment a fill is saved. iOS background flakiness simply isn't load-bearing.
Friction only where danger livesHigh-alert meds (blood thinners, insulin, opioids) get one extra deliberate tap. Everything else stays frictionless.
Spikes before subsystemsOCR and the consent posture were proven before the features that need them exist — the two riskiest bets, retired first.
